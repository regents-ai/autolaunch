## Robinhood bids

A paired agent can prepare its person's USDG bids on Robinhood Chain auctions on this site, and later their return and claim. The site only prepares the steps: your person's own wallet signs and sends each one on Robinhood Chain, and money moves only then. Autolaunch never holds a key and never signs or sends anything.

| Action | WebMCP tool | Hosted MCP tool | HTTP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare a Robinhood bid (signed, paired) | `autolaunch_robinhood_bid_prepare` | `prepare_robinhood_bid` | `POST {{origin}}/api/agent/v1/robinhood/bids/steps` with `auction_id`, `usdg_amount` and `max_price` |
| Prepare a Robinhood bid's return and claim (signed, paired) | `autolaunch_robinhood_bid_settlement_prepare` | `prepare_robinhood_bid_settlement` | `POST {{origin}}/api/agent/v1/robinhood/bids/{bid}/steps` |

Every step is a transaction on Robinhood Chain, the network `chain` names with its `chain_id`, and the wallet must be on that network when it sends one. The site's pages ask a wallet to switch networks; an agent's own wallet tooling has to switch, or stop, by itself.

A bid takes what the auction page takes, as text: `usdg_amount`, the total in USDG with at most 6 decimal places, `max_price`, the most per token in the auction's stock, which is lowered to the auction's price grid, and `slippage_percent` (from 0 to 99.99, 5 when left out). The steps are an exact USDG approval to the bid adapter (`usdg_approval`) while the wallet still needs it, then `usdg_bid`, which converts the USDG into the auction's stock through its admitted route, places the bid and returns what is left over, and reverts if the USDG buys less stock than `facts.min_stock_out` or once fifteen minutes have passed since the block it was built from. `approved` is true when no approval step is needed.

A return and claim takes `bid`, the bid's id as `autolaunch_my_positions` gives it for a bid on Robinhood Chain: the auction's address and the auction's number for the bid, such as `0x…:7`, for one of your person's own bids on an auction listed here. The steps are only those the auction would accept now: `exit`, which returns the unspent stock, then `claim` once the auction graduated and claims are open; `claim` alone for a bid already returned; or `record` while bidding is open and the price has passed the bid's maximum but is not recorded yet, after which the return is prepared again. When nothing can be done yet, the answer is a refusal with the auction's reason.

Every answer gives `kind` (`bid` or `settle`), `signer`, `auction` (`id` and `address`), `chain`, the `block` it was read at, `steps` (`to`, `data` and `value`, each `data` ending with the site's Builder Code) and `facts`; a bid also gives `approved`, and a return and claim its `bid`. Once the auction's record, or the clock the auction keeps at that block, says bidding has ended, a bid is refused with `bidding_ended` and no steps, and while even that clock's next step is before the auction's start block with `auction_not_started`, as the auction is certain to refuse it; one step before the start, the steps come back, as the bid may land in time. Send the steps promptly, and prepare them again once ten minutes have passed, as the auction page builds its own again. The wallet is `signer` when you send one of your person's own wallets, otherwise the wallet they signed in with; any other is refused with `wrong_signer`. Your person needs a verified wallet now, as `autolaunch_my_positions` does; without one, both are refused with `wallet_required`. A bid placed from the wallet your person signed in with joins `autolaunch_my_positions` as soon as it is on the chain; that list reads only that wallet.

A refusal keeps its HTTP status and names its reason: 422 `invalid_request`, `invalid_amount`, `invalid_decimal`, `invalid_price`, `invalid_slippage` or `price_below_admissible_tick` for input to fix; 401 `authentication_required`; 403 `wrong_signer`; 404 `auction_not_found` or `not_your_bid`; 409 `wallet_required`, `bidding_ended`, `bid_preparation_unavailable`, `amount_above_balance`, `usdg_route_unavailable`, `stock_not_listed`, `auction_not_started`, `auction_not_ended`, `already_exited`, `claim_not_open`, `nothing_to_claim`, `bid_not_exited`, `failed_bid_returned` or `settlement_reverted` while the auction or the wallet stands as it is; and 503 `chain_unavailable`, `price_history_long`, `bid_needs_partial_exit_hints_unavailable` or `bid_unavailable`, worth trying again in a moment. The hosted MCP tools at `{{origin}}/mcp` take the same inputs, each under `input`, and give the HTTP answer's `data` as their result; a refusal is their error, with the same code and words. The steps only come back once launches are open. The regents CLI has no bid commands yet.
