## Covered calls

Covered calls on Base stock tokens are open on this site. A seller locks stock tokens and lists calls on them with a strike, a minimum premium and a weekly expiry, always a Friday at 21:00 UTC. Anyone may buy them for the premium in USDC; 1% of each premium goes to REGENT staking. A buyer may instead post a standing bid for calls with a strike, a premium and a weekly expiry, its whole premium held by the contract; a seller who sells stock into it locks that stock as the bidder's calls and is paid the premium, less the 1% fee, at once. A new listing or bid takes one of the next four of these Fridays, leaving out any Friday the US stock market is shut or closes early. Premiums never count toward Points: anyone can buy their own listing or sell into their own bid for only the 1% fee. During the 24 hours after expiry, a holder may pay the strike in USDC and take the stock. A seller is paid a premium only when a buyer buys, in that buyer's own transaction. People use them at [{{origin}}/stock/covered-call]({{origin}}/stock/covered-call).

The exercise window runs from the Friday expiry at 21:00:00 UTC up to, and not including, Saturday at 21:00:00 UTC. Nothing is exercised automatically and no price is checked: calls a holder has not exercised by the close have ended, and their premium is not returned. Exercising pays the strike and moves no stock; the holder then collects the stock with a separate `claim`, which has no deadline. Stock a seller has sold calls on stays locked until the window closes, whatever its price does, and only then can the seller `claim` what was not exercised. The strike paid for exercised calls waits in the contract until the seller sends `collect_strike`. Stock that was never sold comes back with `cancel` and then `claim` at any time before the close; after the close one `claim` sends it with the rest.

Every answer names the stock in `market`: its `symbol`, `issuer`, `chain` and `chain_id`, the `stock` token's address and the covered-calls `contract`'s address.

Every amount is a whole number: stock in raw token units (the book's `unit` is one whole token), prices and USDC in micro-USDC (6 decimals), times in Unix seconds.

A quote gives the reference price, the next four expiries with their exercise windows, and three suggested strikes above the reference price, each with a suggested premium. For the strike and premium you choose (the middle suggestion and its premium when you leave them out) and an `amount`, its `receipt` says what the listing pays if all of it is bought at once and all of it exercised: the premium, the 1% fee, what the seller keeps and the strike, each exactly what the contract counts, so it matches the steps you prepare for the same listing. Beside them, `net_premium_yield` is what the seller keeps as a share of the stock's worth for the term, and `term_days` is that term: the whole days to the expiry.

The hosted MCP tools at `{{origin}}/mcp` take the same inputs, each under `input`, and give the HTTP answer's `data` as their result; a refusal is their error, with the same code and words. Every call there carries your own signature as a paired agent, or a connected app's token once it is paired; `covered_call_positions` and `prepare_covered_call` are for your person's own wallets only.

Your person's covered calls also say how much of the stock their wallet holds (`balance`), the most it can list now: stock already listed is held by the contract. Each of their `holdings` gives the `strike_per_token` to pay and the window it can be exercised in (`expiry` to `exercise_closes_at`). They list every bid their wallet made, with its `stage` (open, sold, cancelled or expired) and what a refund would send it now (`refundable`). A sale into a bid opens a listing like any other, so it shows as the seller's sold listing and the bidder's held calls.

A bid escrows its whole premium when it is posted; the prepared `bid` step's `facts.escrow` is that USDC, and `facts.min_sale` the fewest raw units one sale into it may be (one whole token, or the whole bid when it asks for less), unless a sale takes all that is left. `raise_bid` asks for more at the same terms and escrows only the premium that adds (`facts.escrow`). `sell_to_bid` gives the `premium`, the `fee` and what lands in the seller's wallet (`receive`): all the sales into one bid together pay its premium for all that was sold, rounded up once, and the fee of that total, so each sale takes the next slice of both. `cancel_bid` stops a bid and frees its unspent escrow (`facts.released`); `refund_bid` sends what is due, and after the expiry that includes the escrow of any part never sold.

The site only prepares steps. Each answer lists `steps` (`to`, `data` and `value` on the named `chain`) for one of your person's own wallets: `signer` when you send one, otherwise the wallet they signed in with. That wallet's own key signs and sends the steps in order, and money moves only then. `approved` says whether the allowance already covers the action. `blocked` gives a `code` and words when the chain's current state makes the action certain to fail; the steps still come back, so prepare again once the chain has changed. Any other wallet is refused with `wrong_signer`. The regents CLI has no covered-call commands yet.
