## Agent bids

A paired agent can prepare its person's bids on Base auctions on this site, and later their return and claim. The site only prepares the steps: your person's own wallet signs and sends each one, and money moves only then. Autolaunch never holds a key and never signs or sends anything.

| Action | WebMCP tool | Hosted MCP tool | HTTP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare a bid (signed, paired) | `autolaunch_bid_prepare` | `prepare_bid` | `POST {{origin}}/api/agent/v1/bids/steps` with `auction_id`, `amount` and `max_price` |
| Prepare a bid's return and claim (signed, paired) | `autolaunch_bid_settlement_prepare` | `prepare_bid_settlement` | `POST {{origin}}/api/agent/v1/bids/{bid}/steps` |

A bid takes what the auction page takes, as text: `amount`, the total in the currency paid, and `max_price`, the most per token in the auction's own currency, which is lowered to the auction's price grid. `pay_with: "USDC"` pays in USDC on a Memestock auction that takes it, with `slippage_percent` (from 0 to 99.99, 5 when left out). Paying in the auction's own currency, the steps are an exact approval to Permit2 (`token_approval`) and an exact Permit2 allowance for the auction (`permit2_approval`), each only while the wallet still needs it, then `bid`. Paying in USDC, they are an exact USDC approval to the bid adapter (`usdc_approval`) while needed, then `usdc_bid`, which reverts if the USDC buys less stock than `facts.min_stock_out`. `approved` is true when no approval step is needed.

A return and claim takes `bid`, the bid's id as `autolaunch_my_positions` gives it, for one of your person's own bids on Base. The steps are only those the auction would accept now: `exit`, which returns the unspent money, then `claim` once the auction graduated and claims are open; `claim` alone for a bid already returned; or `record` while bidding is open and the price has passed the bid's maximum but is not recorded yet, after which the return is prepared again. When nothing can be done yet, the answer is a refusal with the auction's reason.

Every answer gives `kind` (`bid` or `settle`), `signer`, `auction` (`id` and `address`), `chain`, the `block` it was read at, `steps` (`to`, `data` and `value`, each `data` ending with the site's Builder Code) and `facts`; a bid also gives `approved`, and a return and claim its `bid`. Once the auction's record or that block says bidding has ended, a bid is refused with `bidding_ended` and no steps, as the auction would refuse it. Send the steps promptly, and prepare them again once ten minutes have passed, as the auction page builds its own again: a Permit2 allowance the steps grant lasts thirty minutes, one the wallet already holds is relied on only with ten minutes left, and a USDC bid is refused fifteen minutes after it was prepared. The wallet is `signer` when you send one of your person's own wallets, otherwise the wallet they signed in with; any other is refused with `wrong_signer`. Your person needs a verified wallet now, as `autolaunch_my_positions` does; without one, both are refused with `wallet_required`. A placed bid joins `autolaunch_my_positions` once the auction's reader records it, within five minutes.

A refusal keeps its HTTP status and names its reason: 422 `invalid_request`, `invalid_amount`, `invalid_decimal`, `invalid_price`, `invalid_slippage` or `price_below_admissible_tick` for input to fix; 401 `authentication_required`; 403 `wrong_signer`; 404 `auction_not_found` or `not_your_bid`; 409 `wallet_required`, `bidding_ended`, `bid_preparation_unavailable`, `treasury_report_missing`, `treasury_security_changed`, `treasury_configuration_drift`, `auction_currency_changed`, `amount_above_balance`, `usdc_bids_unavailable`, `stock_not_admitted`, `usdc_route_unavailable`, `position_not_on_chain`, `bid_not_found`, `auction_not_started`, `auction_not_ended`, `already_exited`, `claim_not_open`, `nothing_to_claim`, `bid_not_exited` or `settlement_reverted` while the auction or the wallet stands as it is; and 503 `chain_unavailable`, `price_history_long`, `bid_needs_partial_exit_hints_unavailable` or `bid_unavailable`, worth trying again in a moment. The hosted MCP tools at `{{origin}}/mcp` take the same inputs, each under `input`, and give the HTTP answer's `data` as their result; a refusal is their error, with the same code and words. The steps only come back once launches are open. The regents CLI has no bid commands yet.
